Omega-3 Intake Calculator

Omega-3 fatty acids are essential polyunsaturated fats that the body cannot produce independently. The three main types are ALA (plant-based), EPA, and DHA (marine-based). EPA and DHA have the strongest research supporting cardiovascular protection, anti-inflammatory effects, brain health, and reduced risk of depression. Most people in Western countries are significantly omega-3 deficient.

Tips to Improve Your Omega-3 Intake

  • Prioritize whole, minimally processed foods for 80% of your intake.
  • Track your intake for at least 2 weeks to understand your real eating patterns.
  • Hydration supports metabolism — aim for pale yellow urine throughout the day.
  • Meal prep on weekends to make healthy choices easier during busy weekdays.
  • Protein at every meal helps with satiety and muscle maintenance.

Frequently Asked Questions About Omega-3 Intake

Fatty fish (salmon, sardines, mackerel), fish oil supplements, flaxseed, chia seeds, walnuts, and algae oil.
ALA is plant-based; EPA and DHA are marine-based. The body converts ALA to EPA/DHA at only 5-10% efficiency, so direct EPA/DHA sources are more effective.
500mg EPA+DHA for general health; 1,000mg for heart health; 2,000-4,000mg for anti-inflammatory or high triglycerides.
If you do not eat fatty fish 2-3 times weekly, high-quality fish oil or algae omega-3 supplements are recommended.
Very high doses (above 5g EPA+DHA) may increase bleeding risk. Doses up to 3g are generally considered safe for most adults.
